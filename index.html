How a transport runs

One call, five checkpoints, a signature at the end.

This is the sequence behind every trip number we issue. It is the same whether you are moving a kidney at 2 a.m. or a tray of instruments on a standing Tuesday route.

  1. 01

    Call comes in

    A dispatcher takes the origin, destination, cargo and hard deadline, then confirms whether the run is emergent.

  2. 02

    Trip number issued

    You get a confirmation number before the unit rolls. It is the reference for every update, document and invoice that follows.

  3. 03

    Custody taken

    Cargo is inspected, sealed where required, and signed for at pickup. The seal number goes on the record with the timestamp.

  4. 04

    Unit tracked

    Computer-aided dispatch follows the vehicle in real time. Status changes push to your coordinator by text or email.

  5. 05

    Delivered and signed

    Receiving staff sign at hand-off. The completed record — times, seals, signatures — closes out under the same trip number.

About Summit

A logistics company that knows what's in the cooler.

Summit Medical Transport Inc. was built around a gap that anyone who has coordinated a recovery has run into: general couriers do not understand medical cargo, and ambulance services are not set up to move equipment and teams. The work needs a carrier that does both, answers at any hour, and can prove where the cargo has been.

That is the whole company. Emergency and non-emergency transport for organs, tissue, equipment and clinical teams — dispatched by people who treat a viability window as a hard constraint, not a delivery estimate.

We are building toward full computer-aided dispatch with advanced vehicle location, a client portal for booking and tracking, and an emergency medical services division. The standard we hold now is the one we intend to scale.
